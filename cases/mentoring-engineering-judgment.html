05 / Technical leadership · Mentoring

Moving review earlier without taking autonomy away

A curious junior engineer found creative technical solutions, but was still developing the judgment to decide when a solution fit the problem. I did not review one approach deeply enough early on; its issues appeared in pre-production and took about three days to untangle.

Context

The engineer had strong curiosity and often knew tools or language features I did not. The gap was in evaluating risk and suitability, not in motivation or raw technical knowledge.

The constraint

The answer could not be to stop delegating difficult work or require every line to look like mine. I needed earlier checkpoints while preserving the engineer’s ability to make decisions.

What I did

  1. For the following sprints, asked the engineer to explain the proposed approach before implementation: choices, alternatives, and assumptions.
  2. Temporarily deepened code review with live walkthroughs of the reasoning behind the implementation.
  3. Used the questions as a scaffold to build independent engineering judgment, and kept the learning two-way.

Outcome

The review point moved from an expensive pre-production discovery to earlier design discussions. This case records a process change, not a quantified reduction in defects.

Trade-off

Closer review took more time initially. It was intended as temporary support, not a permanent approval bottleneck.
